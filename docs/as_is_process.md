# Current Process – AS-IS

## Trigger
During repair or restoration work, an additional issue is identified that was not included in the approved scope.

## Process Steps
1. Additional work is identified by the workshop.
2. Photos or other evidence are collected by the workshop.
3. The required work and cost are estimated by the workshop.
4. The workshop provides the project manager with the relevant technical information.
5. The project manager reviews the information and prepares a clear customer-facing explanation in the customer's language.
6. The project manager contacts the customer and presents the additional work, supporting information and estimated cost.
7. The customer reviews the proposal.
8. The customer either approves the additional work, rejects it or asks for more information.
9. If more information is requested, the project manager gathers and prepares the necessary additional information and sends it to the customer.
10. The customer reviews the updated information and the decision process repeats until the additional work is either approved or rejected.
11. The final customer decision is recorded by the project manager.
12. The project manager communicates the final decision to the workshop.
13. If approved, the additional work is added to the project scope and can proceed.

## Information Used
- Technical information from the workshop
- Photos or other supporting evidence
- Estimated parts cost
- Estimated labor cost
- Total price
- Expected schedule impact, if relevant
- Customer-facing explanation
- Customer questions or requests for additional information
- Additional information provided in response, if required
- Customer decision

## Communication Channels
- Email
- WhatsApp
- Phone

## Decisions
- Approve
- Reject
- Request more information

## Automation Opportunity
The current process works well. The main opportunity is to automate repetitive steps and reduce manual follow-up without changing the existing approval logic.
